# Codex model comparison

The Codex usage investigator's Models view contains a model-comparison panel that starts collapsed. Each side has separate Model, Mode, and Reasoning effort multi-selects, with an all-values checkbox and individual checkboxes. Mode and effort default to all values; the two sides initially select different models when available. Values within a selector are combined, and all three selectors filter together. Unrecorded effort and mode remain individually selectable. Both sides can include overlapping samples, including identical selections. Clearing a selector produces an empty cohort. Combined quantiles are recalculated from the selected original turn samples, never averaged from group quantiles. Switching selections or metrics does not rescan logs or issue IPC calls.

Each option has an “Only” action that replaces the selection in that field with the single value, leaving the other fields and comparison side unchanged. Both sides' filters and the distribution metric are saved locally when changed and restored when reopening the panel or app. All-values selections continue to include newly available values; explicit selections, including empty selections, are preserved across reports. Saved values absent from the current report remain selectable and contribute no samples until available again.

The plot is capped at 42rem wide. On wide panels it sits beside a three-column percentile/A/B table; on narrow panels the plot, table, sides and selector fields stack according to available container width. Long group labels wrap, menu widths stay within the viewport, and the percentile table uses fixed columns with wrapping values. The wide summary table keeps its horizontal scrolling inside the panel.

The chart offers duration, requests, total tokens, output tokens, API-equivalent cost, and Credits. Two lines (solid and dashed) share a scale: the horizontal axis runs from P0 to P100, and the vertical axis uses the selected metric. Each group retains 101 linearly interpolated quantiles, calculated at positions `(n - 1) * percentile / 100` in its sorted usable samples. This is a percentile profile, not a histogram or a probability-density estimate. A table gives exact P0, P10, P25, P50, P75, P90, and P100 quantiles and usable/ended-turn counts. Missing measurements are excluded separately for each metric; zero costs with complete pricing remain valid. A single sample produces a flat line, and an entirely missing metric displays no curve.

The selected-sample details below the chart start collapsed and mount only when expanded. They show the union of the two selected cohorts, marking membership in A, B, or both without duplicate rows. Each row includes its model, mode, effort, completion time in the report display time zone, and all six measurements. Rows are ordered by completion time, newest first, and displayed in pages of 50; changing filters resets pagination. Missing measurements display an em dash, including turns with no usable usage events.

The separately collapsed summary table includes ended-turn counts, median duration, requests, total and output tokens, API-equivalent cost, Codex credits, and cached-input share. Its “Separate reasoning efforts” checkbox remembers the summary grouping preference locally without changing the pair comparison. Hovering a measurement shows its usable sample count. Ending a turn is not evidence of task acceptance or a controlled comparison of equally difficult tasks. Small cohorts and different workloads can distort comparisons even when reasoning effort and service mode match.

Only owned turns from user sessions are included. Inherited fork history, subagent sessions, and source files with malformed or oversized records are excluded. Turns belong to the selected range by completion timestamp; their complete assigned usage is included even if the turn began before the range. Unknown or mixed models are excluded; unrecorded effort and service modes remain distinct groups. Missing duration, usage, or prices are unavailable rather than zero.

Each response is priced before aggregating its turn, preserving event-date Standard API rates and long-context thresholds. Credits retain the observed service-mode multiplier. A turn contributes to a cost median only when every consumed response has a usable price for that measure; a positive cache-write count leaves credits unavailable because the credit rate card has no separate write rate. Subagent costs and external-tool bills are not included.

The main process reads the plugin's existing SQLite cache in bounded pages with cancellation between pages. It retains at most 100,000 completed turns and exports at most 1,000 groups per view; the result marks truncation explicitly. Detailed effort groups also retain compact numeric sample tuples in the order `[completionMs, durationMs, requests, totalTokens, outputTokens, apiEquivalentUsd, codexCredits]`. Model-only summaries do not duplicate these samples. The tuples cross IPC with the cached report and contain no session identifiers, turn identifiers, conversation content, or paths. Collapsed controls and charts are not mounted. This adds no database migration and uses the existing report cache and IPC schema. Reports without sample tuples remain readable and show a refresh prompt for the multi-select comparison and details. On refresh, an older processed snapshot's model comparison is rebuilt from the existing SQLite cache without invalidating the other cached analyses. New CSV exports include both grouping modes, sample coverage, a distribution-method identifier, and a JSON field containing each metric's sample count and P0–P100 values; JSON exports retain both analyses and the numeric samples.

## Credits and subscription percentages

AIY's Credits are an equivalent estimated from local token usage and public model/service-mode rates, not a record of purchased-credit charges. The [official pricing documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing) distinguishes included Plus/Pro limits from credits that extend usage after those limits are reached. It lists Pro allowances at 5x or 20x Plus, depending on the plan, but does not publish a fixed Credits-per-percentage-point conversion. Task complexity, model, context, reasoning, caching and tools all affect usage. A subscription's monthly price is not its included Credits balance.

For a local empirical estimate, align complete usage and quota snapshots for the same account, plan, limit bucket and uninterrupted window: `Credits per percentage point ≈ estimated Credits during the interval / increase in usedPercent`. For example, 40 estimated Credits aligned with a 2-point increase imply about 20 Credits per point in that observed interval. Calibrate five-hour and weekly windows separately; do not mix accounts, plan changes, resets or unrelated activity. This remains an estimate because quota snapshots can lag or be rounded, and local logs may miss cloud or other shared usage. It is not an official conversion or a guarantee for future tasks.
